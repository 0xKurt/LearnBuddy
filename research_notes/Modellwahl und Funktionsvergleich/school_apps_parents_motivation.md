## 1. Per-app features (Germany first, then international) + feature matrix

### Takeaway

German market leaders split into three types: (a) free/cheap, curriculum-shaped practice with light gamification (ANTON, Serlo, Schlaukopf); (b) paid all-in-one "digital tutoring" subscriptions with videos + exercises + test prep + now AI chat (sofatutor, Duden Learnattack, scoyo for younger kids); (c) textbook-bound publisher tools (phase6 vocab per textbook chapter, Klett Lernen, Cornelsen Lernen) and subject specialists (bettermarks, Mathegym). The strongest "hook" for a Gymnasium 6th-grader is **textbook-exact vocabulary and test prep for the next Klassenarbeit**; the parent side is mostly progress reports and, at ANTON, time/game locks.

### Inferences

- [I] The only feature that clearly _wins_ a German Gymnasium 6th-grader's weekly use is **exact alignment with their own textbook and the next test** (phase6 chapter lists, Mathegym/Lernwolf test sheets, sofatutor per Bundesland). Buddy should ask "which book / which chapter / when is the next Klassenarbeit" in chat and prepare from that — plus photo of the textbook page — instead of a curriculum browser.
- [I] ANTON's "lock games for a time" and "pin tasks" are the parent features closest to LearnBuddy's PIN area; LearnBuddy can offer "parent suggests a focus topic" (pinned) without exposing counts to the child.
- [I] Learnattack's WhatsApp photo-homework AI chat is a direct competitor to Buddy's photo flow — and backed by Cornelsen/Duden content.
- [I] Prodigy is a cautionary example for any reward economy: rewards must never be purchasable or visible as status gaps.

## 2. Features loved for exam preparation (Klassenarbeit / test prep)

### Takeaway

Test prep is consistently framed around: the topic list of the coming test (from textbook chapter or curriculum), a simulated test ("Test mode"), and a results view that shows what to repeat. Timed modes are less prominent in the sources than chapter-scoped practice and mock tests.

### Cited Findings

- phase6 markets chapter-sorted vocab explicitly "zum gezielten Lernen für Tests und Klassenarbeiten" [S] — [phase-6.de](https://www.phase-6.de/classic/lerninhalte/vokabeltrainer/cornelsen/)
- Mathegym recommended specifically for "tests and exams" [S] — [lernmarktplatz.de](https://lernmarktplatz.de/pages/lernplattformen-vergleich)
- Quizlet Test mode: "see how you might perform on a test you take in class", choose question types to match how the teacher tests [S] — [Quizlet Test](https://quizlet.com/features/test)
- Kahoot! Test "simulates real exam conditions" [S] — [Kahoot Study](https://kahoot.com/kahoot-study/)
- Seneca: spaced repetition + interleaving for revision [S] — [Seneca help](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483292-what-is-seneca-learning)
- IXL "trouble spots" report = what to repeat before a test [S] — [wiingy.com](https://wiingy.com/blog/ixl-vs-khan-academy/)

### Inferences

- [I] Buddy equivalent: learner says "Mathearbeit am Donnerstag über Brüche" or photographs the teacher's topic list → Buddy builds a short plan backwards from the date (DaySpec), serves spaced practice, then offers one "Probe-Arbeit" at the end with a calm results summary ("sitzt: …, nochmal anschauen: …") — no score counts of missed items, framing as next steps.
- [I] Timed mode should be optional and offered only on request, given anxiety concerns (section 3).

### Gaps

- No German survey data found on which test-prep features students/parents value most.

## 3. Motivation research for ages 10–14: what sustains use without pressure

### Takeaway

Gamification has small-to-moderate positive effects, and SDT suggests autonomy and relatedness matter most for intrinsic motivation; streaks are powerful for retention but produce loss-aversion pressure, and even Duolingo softened them (freezes). Parental involvement helps only when it is autonomy-supportive; monitoring/checking correlates negatively with achievement, and middle school is the age where involvement effects turned negative in one meta-analysis.

### Cited Findings

- Meta-analysis (Sailer & Homner 2020, Educ. Psych. Review): gamification has significant small effects on cognitive (g = .49), motivational (g = .36) and behavioural (g = .25) learning outcomes [S] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)
- Meta-analysis (ETR&D 2023/24): gamification enhances intrinsic motivation and perceived **autonomy and relatedness, but minimal impact on competence**; challenges identified: lack of perceived competence and autonomy in gamified classes [S] — [Springer ETR&D](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)
- Duolingo streaks: industry write-ups claim Streak Freeze reduced churn by 21% among at-risk users, and allowing 2–3 freezes increased DAU retention; these numbers come from secondary blogs/podcast summaries, not Duolingo's primary data (blog.duolingo.com was blocked) [S, secondary] — [yukaichou.com](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-study/master-the-art-of-streak-design-for-short-term-engagement-and-long-term-success/); [Lenny's podcast summary](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team). A claim that next-day retention rose from 12% to 55% via streaks is attributed to a "StriveCloud 2024 analysis" — **dubious, unverified**.
- Streak criticism: streak becomes the goal, learners do the easiest lesson to keep it ("performative learning"), loss aversion; leaderboards motivate some kids and cause anxiety in others. Sources are parent guides/opinion blogs, not peer-reviewed [S] — [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/duolingo-streaks-and-anxiety-in-kids); [Substack essay](https://drracheltaylor.substack.com/p/why-my-daughter-quit-duolingo-the). A master's thesis (Univ. Oulu, 2025) examines streaks and motivation — not fetched — [oulurepo](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/54117/nbnfioulu-202502121605.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)
- Parent homework involvement (Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008, 22 samples/20 studies): positive association in elementary and high school, **negative for middle school** [S] — [Patall et al. 2008](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654308325185)
- Three-level meta-analysis (Psicothema 2024): **autonomy support** improves achievement; content support, control, frequency largely ineffective; monitoring/checking negatively related [S] — [Psicothema PDF](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4826.pdf)
- Hill & Tyson 2009 middle-school meta-analysis (strategies that promote achievement — "academic socialization") exists (not fetched) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2782391/)
- JIM-Studie 2025 (press summaries): many 12–19-year-olds find self-control with the smartphone hard (headline of LFK press release) [S] — [LFK](https://www.lfk.de/service/presse/jim-studie-2025-veroeffentlicht-ki-ist-fester-bestandteil-des-alltags-von-jugendlichen-selbstkontrolle-im-umgang-mit-dem-smartphone-faellt-vielen-schwer)
- BVL guidance: app functions must serve learning; the goal is learning success, "not keeping the child in front of the screen" [S] — [BVL Förderprogramme](https://www.bvl-legasthenie.de/legasthenie/foerderprogramme.html?view=article&id=139&catid=8l)

### Inferences

- [I] Supports LearnBuddy's rules: no streak counters or missed-day counts; prefer autonomy (learner picks topic from 2–3 Buddy suggestions), competence feedback phrased as growth ("Fast richtig — …"), and relatedness via Buddy's persona and memory rather than leaderboards.
- [I] If any continuity signal is used, it should be non-lossable (e.g., "we've covered 4 chapters together" in the parent area only, or a garden/collection that never decays) — a "freeze-by-default" design.
- [I] Parent side should be autonomy-supportive: short weekly narrative ("worked on Brüche, felt confident with…; ask her about …") rather than a monitoring dashboard with minutes and error rates. The Patall middle-school finding argues against giving parents granular checking tools.
- [I] Notifications: combine opt-in, a low cap and quiet hours; frame as invitations, never "you missed…".

### Gaps

- No primary-data study found on notification fatigue specifically in kids' learning apps (searched indirectly; nothing citable).
- Duolingo primary retention data not verified (blog blocked).

## 4. Accessibility and inclusion (Legasthenie/LRS, Dyskalkulie, DaZ, special needs)

### Takeaway

Dyslexia support in apps centres on read-aloud (TTS), clear sans-serif fonts with distinct letterforms and spacing, and not overloading the screen; BVL stresses learning success over engagement. DaZ-oriented apps tested by Stiftung Warentest were mostly weak, the best being free public offers.

### Cited Findings

- Dyslexic readers generally prefer sans-serif fonts with clearly distinguishable letters (a/o, I/l); fonts like Read Regular, Lexie Readable add spacing and distinctive shapes [S] — [Aspose docs](https://docs.aspose.com/font/de/net/what-is-font/dyslexic-fonts/); Austrian font brochure lists OpenDyslexic, Andika, Lexia, Readable, ABeeZee, Tiresias [S] — [legasthenie.at PDF](https://www.legasthenie.at/Fonts_Broschuere.pdf)
- BVL: supplementary learning software is often helpful for reading/writing and arithmetic understanding; several programmes are partly evaluated; functions must serve learning [S] — [BVL](https://www.bvl-legasthenie.de/legasthenie/foerderprogramme.html?view=article&id=139&catid=8l); BVL guide on early detection 2nd ed. 2025 [S] — [BVL PDF](https://www.bvl-legasthenie.de/images/ratgeber/12_Frueherkennung_Foerderung.pdf)
- Specialised LRS apps exist (GoLexic; Kuno app "Lesefenster" reading window) [S] — [GoLexic](https://www.golexic.com/de); [paedalogis Kuno](https://paedalogis.com/kuno-lesefenster)
- Stiftung Warentest German-learning apps: 2 of 12 recommended (BAMF "Ankommen", Goethe "Lern Deutsch – Stadt der Wörter"), 3 of 12 were for children [S] — [test.de](https://www.test.de/Apps-zum-Deutschlernen-Nur-zwei-von-zwoelf-empfehlenswert-4989440-0/)
- ANTON reads task text aloud (useful for weak readers) — [K], not verified this session

### Inferences

- [I] Buddy: TTS for every Buddy message and exercise (tap to hear), a font-choice + spacing option in settings (closed by default), voice input, short sentences; for LRS don't mark spelling errors in non-German subjects as content errors unless it's the learning goal; for dyscalculia offer visual/step-by-step representations and no timers; for DaZ offer simpler German or explanation in a home language (model-supported).
- [I] Avoid claiming "dyslexia-friendly" fonts are proven — evidence on special fonts is mixed [K]; offer them as preference.

### Gaps

- No verified data on how German mainstream apps (sofatutor, Learnattack) implement accessibility.
- Dyscalculia-specific app guidance from BVL not fetched.

## 5. What parents in Germany request / survey data (Bitkom, JIM, reviews)

### Takeaway

Digital learning is daily routine in most German households, parents rate school digitalisation only "befriedigend", and AI is now the default homework tool for teens — but much less among 12–15-year-olds in school. Parents' concerns in reviews: price/contract terms, and reliability of apps.

### Cited Findings

- Bitkom 2025 (1,004 parents of 6–18-year-olds, phone, weeks 22–27/2025): 78% of households — children use digital devices **daily** for learning/school prep; 16% not daily; 4% not at all. 31% report apps/software that don't work; 28% insufficient home bandwidth. Parents grade digital instruction 3.3. 24% want children to learn AI work in school; 62% support mandatory CS [S] — [Bitkom press](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Eltern-vergeben-Note-3-digitalen-Unterricht); [Bitkom Eltern 2025](https://bitkom-research.de/studien/eltern-2025) (PDF blocked)
- Bitkom: ~€500/year spent on the "digital children's room"; one snippet gives €141 average for communication/media access incl. learning apps — the category breakdown is unclear [S] — [Bitkom press](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/500-Euro-jaehrlich-fuer-digitale-Kinderzimmer)
- Bitkom: phone bans at nearly one in five schools [S] — [Bitkom press](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Handyverbot-fuenfte-Schule)
- JIM-Studie 2025 (mpfs, published 14 Nov 2025): **74% of 12–19-year-olds use AI for homework or learning** (2024: 65%); AI use for information search 70% (+27 pp); ChatGPT is second after search engines for research; 57% find AI information trustworthy; AI use _in school_: 63% of 16–19 vs **39% of 12–15** [S] — [bildungsklick](https://bildungsklick.de/schule/detail/jim-studie-2025-mediennutzung-ki); [GEW](https://www.gew.de/aktuelles/detailseite/ki-wird-vor-allem-fuer-hausaufgaben-genutzt); [Internet-ABC](https://www.internet-abc.de/eltern/studien-zu-mediennutzung-kinder-jugendliche/jim-studie-2025-ki-im-alltag-von-jugendlichen/) (mpfs.de itself blocked)
- sofatutor reviews: price too high, inflexible contract [S] — [trusted.de](https://trusted.de/sofatutor)

### Inferences

- [I] For parents, the purchase arguments are: fits the child's school/book, no ads, fair and cancellable pricing, visible (but not controlling) progress, and safe AI use (not just answering homework). Buddy's "explain, don't do the homework" behaviour is a parent-facing selling point given 74% AI homework use.
- [I] "Works reliably" matters (31% report broken apps) — offline-tolerant practice is worth considering.

- No German survey found that ranks parents' requested features (reports, time limits, etc.).

## 6. Machine-readable German curriculum data

### Takeaway

KMK Bildungsstandards are available machine-readable (SKOS, via IQB on GitHub), but these are national standards for grade 4/9/10/Abitur competencies, not per-grade/per-Bundesland topic lists. Länder Lehrpläne are mostly published only as PDFs; a DINI-AG-KIM draft data model for curricula exists but coverage is prototypical.

### Cited Findings

- IQB publishes the KMK Bildungsstandards modelled in SKOS at iqb-vocabs.github.io; each vocabulary group lives in a GitHub repo as CSV, transformed to Turtle with IQB's csv2ttl, published with SkoHub; the org has ~83 repos (e.g. Bildungsstandard-Deutsch-Primar, Bildungsstandard-Deutsch-Sek-I) [S] — [iqb-vocabs.github.io](https://iqb-vocabs.github.io/vokabulare/); [GitHub iqb-vocabs](https://github.com/iqb-vocabs); [iqb-berlin/mdc-educational-standards](https://github.com/iqb-berlin/mdc-educational-standards); [DINI blog 2024](https://blog.dini.de/EPub_FIS/2024/04/10/oer-standards-kim/)
- There is no uniform data-exchange model for Lehrpläne; they are mostly published as PDF; a prototypical model ("Datenmodell zur Abbildung von Lehrplänen", DINI AG KIM, draft) was developed; the working group also serves projects It's Jointly and MEM [S] — [DINI AG KIM draft](https://dini-ag-kim.github.io/modell_lehrplaene/draft/) (blocked); [Jointly 2020](https://its.jointly.info/2020/09/25/maschinenlesbare-lehrplaene/)
- Switzerland's lehrplan.ch offers an API on request [S] — [Jointly](https://jointly.info/2020/08/12/maschinenlesbare-lehrplaene-wofuer/)

### Inferences

- [I] For grade 6 Gymnasium topic mapping, the practical source is the learner's own textbook/chapter (via photo or book title) rather than Lehrplan data; Bildungsstandards SKOS could be used as a coarse competence taxonomy (tags) for Deutsch/Mathe/Englisch/Französisch — check the licence in each repo (a LICENSE file exists for Deutsch-Sek-I; terms not read).
- [I] Per-Bundesland grade-6 topic lists would have to be curated by hand (e.g., from Bavaria's LehrplanPLUS website, which is HTML structured [K]) — cost vs. benefit is questionable when Buddy can read the textbook page.

### Gaps

- Whether IQB SKOS covers Sek-I for Mathe, Englisch, Französisch, Physik (not verified per repo); licences not read.
- Whether any Bundesland (e.g. Bayern LehrplanPLUS, BW Bildungsplan 2016) offers an API or export — not verified.
